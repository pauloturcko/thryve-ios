# UX and Interaction-Design Principles for Signaling Voice Capture States (late 2026)

Context for the report writer: the target is a minimalist, monochrome iOS app (white on black / black on white) with one central element. The user taps it, speaks an expense, and the app transcribes and saves it. Recent commits suggest on-device Apple speech and Apple Intelligence extraction. Sources are 2023–2026 unless marked **[Classic, pre-2023]**. Where a claim comes only from a search-result summary and not from a page I fetched, it is marked *(snippet-level)*.

---

## 1. Which states should a voice capture UI communicate, and how do leading products tell them apart (visuals, motion, haptics)?

### Takeaway
Current practitioner guidance and shipped products agree: design the state machine before the animation. Each visible state must follow a real system signal, not an optimistic button press. Keep the *semantic state* (idle / connecting / listening / processing / done / error) separate from the *audio-level motion* (how much the element moves with the voice). Leading products tell states apart mainly by changing the *kind* of motion (reactive vs self-driven vs still), plus shape, text and sound. Color alone is not enough, which suits a monochrome app.

### Cited Findings
- orb-ui, a React voice-agent UI library, defines six lifecycle states: **idle** (no session; user can start one), **connecting** ("Progress should not imply the microphone is active"), **listening** (app is receiving user audio), **thinking** (user's turn ended, agent processing), **speaking**, and **error** ("the user needs a message and a recovery path"). — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)
- orb-ui says to design transitions before visuals, and to show `listening` "only once the session can receive input". Its warnings: "Do not treat a click as proof that a voice session started" and "The visible state should follow the session, not the optimistic button press." — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)
- orb-ui: "State is the semantic layer. Volume is the motion layer." `inputVolume` should follow the local mic while listening, and the two layers should stay separate. It lists `interrupted`, `muted` and `transcribing` as future signals, to be added "only when they remove real ambiguity". — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)
- orb-ui's theme docs frame listening as answering "did the app hear me?" and thinking as "is the assistant processing?". They suggest calm idle motion and more responsive motion in active states. — [orb-ui: Voice states](https://orb-ui.com/docs/themes/voice-states) *(snippet-level)*
- An open-source WebGL orb (orb-voice-visualizer) has idle / listening / thinking / speaking states that blend over about 400 ms. The orb is driven by mic level and labels the element with its current state for accessibility. — [GitHub: orb-voice-visualizer](https://github.com/YagnikBarasiya23/orb-voice-visualizer) *(snippet-level; hobbyist project, low authority)*
- A Google patent on an "LED design language for visual affordance of voice user interfaces" gives listening and thinking separate light patterns: brightness that varies during listening, and breathing or flickering motion for processing states. — [USPTO 10304450](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10304450); [USPTO 10861461](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10861461) *(snippet-level; patent, not a shipped spec)*
- **Wispr Flow**: the Flow Bar is "a small floating bubble showing your dictation status: resting, recording, or processing." — [Wispr Flow docs: What's the bar at the bottom of my screen?](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/faqs/curious-questions) *(snippet-level)*
- Wispr Flow's listening state shows moving white bars that follow input volume, like a minimal VU meter, plus a start ping. Help-center copy: "When you hear a ping or see the white bars moving, start speaking." No separate "thinking" spinner is documented; the text appearing at the cursor acts as the success signal. Recordings under about 0.5 s are discarded ("audio saves only if you spoke for at least half a second"). — [Third-party compiled Wispr UX reference (cites Wispr help center for the ping, bars and 0.5 s rule; processing-state details are the compiler's inference)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/wispr-ux.md)
- **Siri (Apple Intelligence, iOS 18.1+)** replaced the orb with "an edge-lit animation which adapts in response to the sound of your voice." — [9to5Mac, Nov 2024](https://9to5mac.com/2024/11/03/new-apple-intelligence-siri-looks-different-works-the-same/). MacRumors describes a variegated glow around the whole screen, "with the colors shifting as Siri listens to a command." — [MacRumors Siri guide](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/siri-apple-intelligence/). Devices without Apple Intelligence keep the classic orb. — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1865686/iphone-glowing-around-edges-reason/)
- **ChatGPT**: Advanced Voice Mode shipped with a blue animated sphere, replacing the animated black dots shown in the May 2024 demo. — [TechCrunch, Sep 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/24/openai-rolls-out-advanced-voice-mode-with-more-voices-and-a-new-look). In Nov 2025 voice moved into the main chat window, and the separate full-screen orb became an optional setting. — [TechCrunch, Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/25/chatgpts-voice-mode-is-no-longer-a-separate-interface/); [MacRumors, Nov 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/11/26/chatgpt-voice-mode-update-seamless-chat/)
- **Gemini Live**: as of an Oct 27, 2026 APK teardown, the audio visualizer uses a static pill shape. Google is testing a "Neural Expressive" version, "a circular button surrounded by animated dots and a glowing blue background." It is unreleased and may not ship, and the article does not say how listening, speaking and thinking are told apart. — [Android Authority, Oct 2026](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-live-neural-expressive-audio-visualizer-redesign-3728666/)
- Gemini's visual identity is built on gradients and "the fundamental shape of the circle." — [Google Design: Illustrating the Gemini App](https://design.google/library/gemini-ai-visual-design). A 9to5Google write-up says the post calls motion an "essential guiding element." — [9to5Google, Jan 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/01/17/gemini-gradient-design/) *(snippet-level)*
- Apple HIG, haptics: "Use system-provided haptic patterns according to their documented meanings." The **notification** patterns are Success ("a task or action has completed"), Warning and Error. **Impact** patterns (light / medium / heavy / rigid / soft) are "a physical metaphor you can use to complement a visual experience." — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- Apple HIG, haptics: "build a clear, causal relationship between each haptic and the action that causes it", and "In most apps, prefer playing short haptics that complement discrete events." Long-running haptics "can dilute the meaning of the feedback." — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- **Implementation constraint (important):** Apple HIG says to "Ensure that haptic vibrations don't disrupt experiences involving device features like the camera, gyroscope, or microphone." — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics). In practice, iOS disables haptics and system sounds by default while an audio session is recording, to keep them out of the recording. `AVAudioSession.setAllowHapticsAndSystemSoundsDuringRecording(true)` (iOS 13+, default `false`) overrides this. One developer found the call was ignored unless it came after the capture session was configured. — [DEV: Haptic feedback and AVAudioSession conflicts](https://dev.to/maxnxi/haptic-feedback-and-avaudiosession-conflicts-in-ios-troubleshooting-recording-issues-2ocl); [Zenn (fromkk)](https://zenn.dev/fromkk/articles/c79ebea77a813b); [Apple Developer Forums: Haptic feedback at start of audio recording](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/39543)
- **[Classic, pre-2023]** NN/g's analysis of the Amazon Echo: "Alexa's visibility of system status is limited to an animated light ring; while this is well executed, it's a far cry from the rich textual feedback possible with a screen." — [NN/g voice topic hub](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/voice/) *(snippet-level; exact article URL not retrieved)*. A heuristics article adds that the user should know when the system is listening, processing and speaking. — [UX Collective: Nielsen's heuristics applied to VUI](https://uxdesign.cc/10-usability-heuristics-for-voice-user-interface-design-69ad9ea4f166) *(snippet-level)*

### Inferences
- **Recommended state set for a one-shot expense capture** (no "speaking" state, since the app doesn't talk back):
  1. **Idle/ready**: at rest, inviting a tap.
  2. **Permission needed**: a different glyph or shape plus short text, not just a dimmed orb.
  3. **Preparing**: the tap has landed but the audio engine isn't live yet. Show press feedback, not "listening".
  4. **Listening, hearing speech**: the element reacts to input level.
  5. **Listening, silence**: the element settles to a living baseline, not a frozen one, so the user knows the mic is still open.
  6. **Processing**: transcription is final and the AI is extracting the expense.
  7. **Saved/success**.
  8. **No speech detected**.
  9. **Error** (recognition, model or save failure).
- **How to tell states apart in monochrome:** use the *kind* of motion (still → slow breathing → audio-reactive → self-driven shimmer or rotation → settle), the *geometry* (filled disc vs ring vs morph to a check mark), *scale*, *opacity*, and a short **text label**. Color is out, both by design and by guidance.
- "Speech vs silence" works best as the audio-level layer *inside* the listening state, not as a separate semantic state. Amplitude drives intensity; silence decays to a gentle baseline.
- Haptic mapping per HIG: a light or soft **impact** on tap (before the mic opens), a **success notification** when the expense is saved, and an **error notification** on failure. "No speech" could reuse warning or a soft impact. Because iOS mutes haptics while recording, either fire the start haptic *before* activating the record session and the end haptics *after* deactivating it, or opt in with `setAllowHapticsAndSystemSoundsDuringRecording(true)` and test on device.

### Gaps
- I found no official design write-up from OpenAI, Google or Apple explaining *how* their voice visuals encode listening vs thinking. Product descriptions come from press and teardowns.
- I found no quantitative study comparing state encodings (shape vs motion vs text) for voice UIs.
- The Apple HIG "Siri" and "Audio" pages were not retrieved this session (Apple HIG pages render with JavaScript; the JSON endpoints worked for Motion, Privacy, Accessibility and Playing haptics only).

## 2. Turn-taking and end-of-speech cues: push-to-talk vs tap-to-toggle vs automatic silence detection; signaling "I stopped listening"; live transcription display

### Takeaway
Hold-to-talk gives the user exact control over the end of the turn. Tap-to-toggle frees the hand but depends on an explicit second tap or silence detection. Best-in-class dictation (Wispr Flow) offers both, and a cheap hybrid is to read a long press as hold-to-talk and a short tap as toggle. For live text, Apple's 2025 SpeechAnalyzer API separates fast "volatile" guesses from "finalized" text. The common UI pattern is volatile text at reduced emphasis, upgraded in place when finalized, which maps cleanly to monochrome opacity.

### Cited Findings
- AssemblyAI guide: push-to-talk ends the turn immediately on button release; timeout-based systems end it after a fixed silence. Running voice-activity endpoints and model-based turn detection together can "race" to close the same turn. For its streaming model it recommends a 100 ms minimum silence (lower values can split multi-word entities), and says older 400 ms defaults can feel slow in quick conversational exchanges. — [DEV / AssemblyAI: Turn detection vs forced endpoints](https://dev.to/martschweiger/turn-detection-vs-forced-endpoints-in-voice-ai-why-getting-this-wrong-tanks-your-ux-4m1g) *(snippet-level; vendor guidance for conversational agents, not dictation)*
- LiveAvatar's push-to-talk mode hands start/stop to the application "rather than the system guessing", and recommends mapping it to press-and-hold, toggle or keyboard shortcuts. — [LiveAvatar docs: Push to talk](https://docs.liveavatar.com/docs/push-to-talk) *(snippet-level)*
- A patent family, "Adaptive communication mode for recording a media message", describes one control that infers the mode from behavior: hold while speaking means push-to-talk; press then release in silence means tap-to-start. Lower and upper hold-time thresholds decide which. — [USPTO 8996059](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8996059) *(snippet-level; patent)*
- Wispr Flow supports both modes: hold a hotkey and release to insert ("Hold a key or mouse button to dictate"), or double-tap to start and stop hands-free ("Double-tap to start and stop dictation without holding"). `Esc` cancels without inserting. Stopping is shown visually (the bars stop moving, then the text pastes); no stop sound is documented. — [Compiled Wispr UX reference citing Wispr help center](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/wispr-ux.md)
- Wispr's designer says the team "optimized the hold-and-speak interaction" and "prioritized responsiveness over visible feedback". Flow does not stream word-by-word text while you speak; it can "wait, understand, and then write what you *meant*", which "allows Flow to be less distracting while you're speaking." — [Wispr: Designing a natural and useful voice interface (Aditya Mankare, Jul 18 2025)](https://wisprflow.ai/post/designing-a-natural-and-useful-voice-interface)
- User demand for toggle: a GitHub issue on Claude asks for click-to-toggle instead of press-and-hold-only voice input, and claims macOS Voice Control and Whisper-based dictation tools default to toggle. — [GitHub anthropics/claude-code #53411](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/53411) *(user request; claims unverified)*
- Apple WWDC25 "SpeechAnalyzer" (session 277): **volatile results** arrive "almost as soon as they're spoken" but are less accurate guesses, refined as more audio arrives. They appear only when you opt in; by default the transcriber delivers only **finalized** results. Once a range of audio is finalized, no more results come for that range. — [WWDC25 session 277: Bring advanced speech-to-text to your app with SpeechAnalyzer](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/)
- A developer guide recommends: request `.volatileResults`, "Show volatile dimmed, replace it on a final, persist only finals." — [DEV: iOS 26's SpeechAnalyzer on a live mic](https://dev.to/simple_memo/ios-26s-speechanalyzer-on-a-live-mic-the-5-things-the-docs-dont-tell-you-2ng5) *(snippet-level; third-party)*
- ChatGPT's Nov 2025 update shows voice responses as live text inside the chat, rather than only an orb on a separate screen. — [TechCrunch, Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/25/chatgpts-voice-mode-is-no-longer-a-separate-interface/); [MacRumors, Nov 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/11/26/chatgpt-voice-mode-update-seamless-chat/)
- orb-ui on interruption: stop output motion as soon as playback stops. More generally, the visible state must follow the real session. — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)

### Inferences
- For one short expense utterance ("café 12 reais"), **tap-to-toggle with silence auto-stop as the main path, plus a manual tap-to-stop**, is the natural fit. A press held past a threshold could switch to hold-to-talk (the patent's hybrid) for power users or noisy places.
- Pause tolerance should be longer than the 100–400 ms conversational turn-detection values above, since people pause mid-phrase while recalling an amount. The exact number has to be tuned by testing.
- **"I stopped listening"** should be a clear, single transition: the element stops reacting to audio *immediately* (the audio-level layer drops out), changes geometry (e.g. contracts or morphs from a reactive disc into the processing form), and may play a soft end haptic once the record session is closed. This matters for trust: motion must never suggest listening after the mic is closed.
- **Live transcript in monochrome:** show volatile words at reduced opacity (e.g. secondary label color) and switch to full opacity when finalized. Keep text short and under or near the central element. Then replace the raw transcript with the parsed result (amount, category) on success, so the user confirms *what was saved*, not just what was heard.
- Wispr's no-live-text stance works for long-form dictation where text is cleaned up afterwards. For an expense logger, where the amount must be right, a live or final transcript gives a check point before saving. This is a product trade-off to test.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled UX study (2023–2026) comparing push-to-talk, toggle and auto-endpointing for mobile dictation.
- I found no published silence-timeout values for short-form *dictation* (as opposed to conversational agents), and no Apple guidance on default end-of-speech behavior for SpeechAnalyzer in UI terms.

## 3. Trust and privacy signaling: recording indicators, the iOS orange mic dot, on-device processing cues

### Takeaway
iOS already draws a system-level orange dot whenever any app uses the microphone, and an app can neither suppress nor restyle it. The app's job is to make its own visuals *agree* with that dot: never show listening while the mic is off, and stop as soon as it closes. Apple's privacy guidance favors asking for permission in context, writing specific active-voice purpose strings, and processing on device where possible, which gives the app a truthful "on-device" message.

### Cited Findings
- "An orange indicator means the microphone is being used by an app on your iPhone." — [Apple Support: About the orange and green indicators in your iPhone status bar](https://support.apple.com/108331)
- Apple Community answers say Control Center shows the mic or camera icon and which app used it most recently, though some users report it doesn't always identify the app. — [Apple Community thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256177990) *(snippet-level; user forum, not Apple guidance)*
- Apple HIG, Privacy: "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access to the data or resource… Ideally, wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature that requires access." Also: "Avoid requesting permission at launch unless the data or resource is required for your app to function." — [Apple HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- Apple HIG, pre-permission screens: you may show a custom screen before the system alert, but "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert" (e.g. "Continue" or "Next"). Don't add a cancel/close path, and don't imitate the alert or use "Allow" on your own button. — [Apple HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- Apple HIG, purpose strings: "Aim for a brief, complete sentence that's straightforward, specific, and easy to understand… avoid passive voice." Good example: "The app records during the night to detect snoring sounds." Bad example: "Microphone access is needed for a better experience." — [Apple HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- Apple HIG: "Process data on the device where possible… helping you avoid lengthy and potentially risky round trips to a remote server." — [Apple HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- orb-ui: the connecting state's "Progress should not imply the microphone is active," and the visible state should follow the session, not the optimistic button press. — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)
- Wispr Flow shows a persistent, small resting indicator ("a small, persistent bar on the dock that signals availability without noise") and avoids flashing "an obstructive UI just to remind you it exists". Wispr's cleanup runs in the cloud. — [Wispr design post (Jul 2025)](https://wisprflow.ai/post/designing-a-natural-and-useful-voice-interface); [compiled Wispr UX reference](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/wispr-ux.md)
- Latency of cloud models was a central complaint about 2024 AI gadgets (Humane AI Pin, Rabbit R1). IEEE Spectrum attributed it to the best LLMs residing in data centers and argued the devices launched before on-device models matured. — [IEEE Spectrum: AI gadgets 2024](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-gadgets-2024)
- **[Classic, pre-2023]** NN/g found that even phone-based assistants raised trust issues, despite having a screen to show supporting evidence. — [Boing Boing summary of NN/g study](https://boingboing.net/2018/07/24/sirius-cybernetics-corp.html/amp) *(snippet-level, secondary)*; [NN/g: Intelligent Assistants Have Poor Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intelligent-assistant-usability/)

### Inferences
- **Mirror the orange dot.** The app's "listening" visual should start when the audio engine starts and stop the moment it stops, so users never see the dot without the listening visual or the reverse. That match is the strongest trust signal a minimal UI can give.
- Ask for mic (and speech recognition) permission on the **first tap of the central element**, not at launch. An optional one-button pre-screen can explain it. Example purpose string in active voice: "Thryve listens only while you hold or tap the button, to turn what you say into an expense." Localize it to pt-BR.
- If transcription and extraction really run on device (SpeechAnalyzer + Foundation Models), a small one-time line such as "Processed on your iPhone" (e.g. in onboarding or under the first result) is a truthful cue in Apple's own language. It should not be claimed if any step goes to a server.
- Keep a permanent, low-key "ready" state rather than an always-animated element. An always-moving mic metaphor can read as "always listening".

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring how users perceive in-app recording indicators alongside the iOS orange dot.
- I found no Apple guidance on wording "on-device" claims inside apps beyond the HIG principle quoted above.
- Humane AI Pin's "Trust Light" (a recording indicator) was not covered by the sources I retrieved.

## 4. Motion design principles: latency targets, easing/springs, avoiding jitter, idle "breathing", delight vs distraction; Apple HIG; Material 3 Expressive

### Takeaway
Apple's HIG motion guidance is restrained: motion should be purposeful, brief and precise, cancellable, and kept to a minimum on frequent interactions. For voice that means the central element must respond within about 100 ms of the tap, never block input, and avoid ornamental motion. Audio-reactive motion should change *intensity* (scale, glow, opacity), not trigger layout movement. Idle motion should be slow and low contrast, or absent. Material 3 Expressive moved Google's system to spring physics. Official spring tokens weren't retrieved, but the principle (physics-based, interruptible motion) matches SwiftUI's default springs.

### Cited Findings
- Apple HIG, Motion: "Add motion purposefully, supporting the experience without overshadowing it." "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion. Gratuitous or excessive animation can distract people and may make them feel disconnected or physically uncomfortable." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG: "Aim for brevity and precision in feedback animations. When animated feedback is brief and precise, it tends to feel lightweight and unobtrusive, and it can often convey information more effectively than prominent animation." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG: "In apps, generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently… avoid making people spend extra time paying attention to unnecessary motion every time they interact with it." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG: "Let people cancel motion. As much as possible, don't make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything, especially if they have to experience the animation more than once." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG: "Strive for realistic feedback motion that follows people's gestures and expectations." It also points to animated SF Symbols (SF Symbols 5+) "where it makes sense". For visionOS it warns against oscillation around 0.2 Hz. — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG, haptics: "match the intensity and sharpness of a haptic with the intensity and sharpness of the animation it accompanies"; "When visual, auditory, and tactile feedback are in harmony… the user experience is more coherent." — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- orb-ui motion rules: "Keep idle motion slow and low contrast. Use audio levels to change intensity, not to trigger layout movement. Avoid flashes and rapid oscillation." And: "The visual state should change quickly enough to feel alive, but it should not distract from the conversation." — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)
- Wispr: "Designing invisible interfaces is not about being minimal"; "It's why we sweated the latency and the interaction"; "Flow should disappear into your muscle memory." — [Wispr design post (Jul 2025)](https://wisprflow.ai/post/designing-a-natural-and-useful-voice-interface)
- Voice latency benchmark: GPT-4o "can respond to audio inputs in as little as 232 milliseconds, with an average of 320 milliseconds, which is similar to human response time in a conversation." The earlier three-model pipeline averaged 2.8 s (GPT-3.5) and 5.4 s (GPT-4). — [OpenAI: Hello GPT-4o (May 2024)](https://openai.com/index/hello-gpt-4o/)
- **[Classic, pre-2023]** Nielsen's response-time limits (1993, restated 2014): 0.1 s is the limit for users "feeling that they are directly manipulating objects in the UI"; 1.0 s is about the limit for the user's flow of thought to stay uninterrupted; beyond that, show that the system is working. — [NN/g: Response Times: The 3 Important Limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) (primary, not retrieved); [secondary summary, jmduke](https://www.jmduke.com/posts/response-times.md)
- Material 3 Expressive (2025): community and third-party implementations describe it as replacing duration/cubic-bezier easing with spring physics tuned by stiffness and damping, with a spring matrix of **spatial vs effects** × **fast / default / slow**. Spatial springs (position, size) may overshoot; effects springs (color, opacity) should not. — [expressive_m3 Flutter package](https://pub.dev/packages/expressive_m3); [M3E motion skill summary](https://skills.sh/ahmed3elshaer/everything-claude-code-mobile/m3-expressive) *(snippet-level; secondary)*. Google's Wear OS guide says motion "spatially connects surfaces". — [Android Developers: Wear OS Material 3 Expressive](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started) *(snippet-level)*

### Inferences
- **Latency budget:** (a) visual press feedback on the element within ~100 ms of touch-down (optimistic *press* feedback is fine; optimistic *listening* is not); (b) if starting the audio engine takes noticeably longer, show a distinct "preparing" look so the user doesn't start talking into a closed mic; (c) from end of speech to saved result, aim to stay within ~1 s, and past that, the processing state must clearly show work in progress.
- **Springs:** use critically damped or lightly bouncy springs for spatial changes (scale and morphs between states), and plain non-bouncy transitions for opacity or brightness (the M3 "effects" rule). Springs are interruptible by nature, which fits the HIG's "let people cancel motion". Tapping during processing or success must not wait for an animation to finish.
- **Avoiding jitter in audio-reactive motion:** smooth the raw mic level before driving visuals (attack/release envelope, light low-pass) and map it to a narrow scale range, so the element "breathes with" the voice instead of shaking. Separate state transitions (springs) from level modulation (continuous, smoothed). This applies the orb-ui "semantic vs motion layer" rule.
- **Idle breathing:** acceptable only if very slow, low amplitude and low contrast. Because the central element is tapped constantly (a frequent interaction, per HIG), a static or barely moving idle state is the safer premium choice. Save the expressive motion for listening, where it carries information (proof the mic hears you).
- The success animation should be short and precise (a morph to a check mark plus the parsed amount), paired with the success haptic at matching sharpness. Avoid celebratory effects that slow down repeated logging.

### Gaps
- The official M3 Expressive spring token values (m3.material.io) and Google's published Expressive research findings were not retrieved. Any specific stiffness, damping or research numbers need to come from m3.material.io directly.
- I found no Apple-published target for "tap to listening" latency, and no study on breathing idle animations for voice UIs.
- I found no source on audio-level smoothing constants (attack/release times) for voice visualizers.

## 5. Accessibility: Reduce Motion alternatives, VoiceOver announcements, color-independent cues, haptics

### Takeaway
Apple's accessibility HIG gives concrete Reduce Motion substitutions (tighter springs, fades instead of movement, fewer repeated or automatic animations). It requires state to be conveyed by more than color, and asks that audio cues be paired with haptics and visuals. For voice, never make the animation the only state signal: add visible status text, a VoiceOver label and value, and announcements via SwiftUI's `AccessibilityNotification.Announcement`.

### Cited Findings
- Apple HIG, Accessibility (Reduce Motion): "When this setting is active, ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion." Best practices include "Tightening animation springs to reduce bounce effects," "Tracking animations directly with people's gestures," "Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades to avoid motion," and "Avoiding animating into and out of blurs." — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Apple HIG, Accessibility: "Convey information with more than color alone… Offer visual indicators, like distinct shapes or icons, in addition to color to help people perceive differences in function and changes in state." — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Apple HIG, Accessibility: "Use haptics in addition to audio cues… consider pairing that sound with matching haptics for people who can't perceive the audio or have their audio turned off." And "Augment audio cues with visual cues." — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Apple HIG, Accessibility: "Be cautious with fast-moving and blinking animations… it can be distracting, cause dizziness, and in some cases even result in epileptic episodes." — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Apple HIG, Accessibility: minimum contrast follows WCAG AA (4.5:1 up to 17 pt; 3:1 at 18 pt or bold). iOS controls should be 44×44 pt by default (28×28 pt minimum). Support Increase Contrast if defaults fall short, and check both light and dark appearances. — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Apple HIG, Motion: "Make motion optional… avoid using it as the only way to communicate important information… supplement visual feedback by also using alternatives like haptics and audio." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- Apple HIG, haptics: "Make haptics optional. Let people turn off or mute haptics, and make sure people can still enjoy your app or game without them." — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- SwiftUI (WWDC23): `AccessibilityNotification.Announcement("…").post()` sends a VoiceOver announcement, and announcements can be given priority: "the high priority announcement does interrupt the default and low announcements." — [WWDC23 session 10036: Build accessible apps with SwiftUI and UIKit](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10036/). Announcements only speak when VoiceOver (or braille) is active. — [Apple docs: NSAccessibility announcementRequested](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsaccessibility-swift.struct/notification/announcementrequested.md) *(snippet-level)*. Developers report inconsistent announcement delivery, especially right after screen-change notifications. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 705766](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/705766) *(snippet-level)*
- orb-ui accessibility rules: "A voice visualization should never be the only way to understand the session. Expose visible status text when state matters." Give interactive orbs an accessible name, keep start/stop operable without touch gestures, and respect reduced-motion preferences. — [orb-ui: Voice Agent UI guide](https://orb-ui.com/docs/guides/voice-agent-ui)
- A voice-orb implementation spec (GitHub issue) lists as acceptance criteria that animation must reflect actual state, respect reduced motion, and that states must be distinguishable without relying only on animation. — [GitHub: wheelhouse issue #71](https://github.com/Carlsmeister/wheelhouse/issues/71) *(snippet-level; low authority, but shows a converging practice)*

### Inferences
- **Monochrome is fine here**, since the HIG already requires color-independent cues. The state vocabulary should be geometry (disc / ring / check / exclamation glyph), scale, opacity and a one- or two-word status label ("Ouvindo…", "Processando…", "Salvo", "Não entendi"). Contrast between white and black is maximal; check that any secondary-opacity text, such as volatile transcript, still clears 4.5:1.
- **Reduce Motion version:** swap the audio-reactive scaling for a non-scaling cue, such as opacity or stroke-weight changes, or a small static level indicator. Use crossfades between states instead of morphs and scale bursts. Drop idle breathing entirely. Keep the haptics and text unchanged.
- **VoiceOver:** the central element needs an accessibility label (e.g. "Registrar gasto por voz"), a value that changes with state ("Ouvindo", "Processando", "Salvo: R$ 12, Café"), and a hint ("Toque duas vezes para começar a falar"). Post announcements on processing → saved and on errors. **Caution (inference, unverified):** a VoiceOver announcement spoken *while the mic is recording* may be picked up by the mic and transcribed. Announce state changes after capture ends, or rely on haptics and earcons at the start. This needs device testing.
- Pair every key transition with at least two channels: (start: haptic + visual + optional earcon), (saved: success haptic + check mark + text), (error: error haptic + glyph + text with recovery action). This covers deaf, blind, low-vision and Reduce Motion users at the same time.

### Gaps
- I found no Apple guidance specific to VoiceOver behavior while an app is actively recording audio.
- I found no research on how users with Reduce Motion perceive audio-reactive visualizers.

## 6. What separates premium voice UIs from generic ones (ChatGPT voice, Siri, Gemini Live, Wispr Flow, Humane, Rabbit)?

### Takeaway
The sources show premium voice UIs share four traits:
- **Responsiveness:** perceived latency is the product. Wispr "sweated the latency", GPT-4o cut voice latency from seconds to about 320 ms, and Humane and Rabbit were punished for slowness.
- **Honesty:** visuals tied to real audio and session signals.
- **Restraint:** the "invisible" UI, which is not the same as merely minimal.
- **Coherence:** visual, haptic and audio feedback in harmony.

Generic UIs decorate: always-on animation, color-only states, spinners with no state meaning, and modes that confuse. A recent trend among leaders is to move *away* from the spectacle screen toward voice inline with content (ChatGPT, Nov 2025; Gemini Live tests in 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **Wispr Flow (premium dictation reference):** "Designing invisible interfaces is not about being minimal"; "We also didn't want to constantly flash an obstructive UI just to remind you it exists"; "Why we prioritized responsiveness over visible feedback"; "Flow should disappear into your muscle memory." — [Wispr: Designing a natural and useful voice interface (Aditya Mankare, Jul 2025)](https://wisprflow.ai/post/designing-a-natural-and-useful-voice-interface). The Flow Bar can be moved and docked. — [Wispr docs: Move and dock the Flow Bar](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/1790396454-move-and-dock-the-flow-bar-on-desktop)
- **ChatGPT voice:** went from black-dot demo to blue sphere (2024), then moved voice into the main chat with an inline transcript, keeping the separate orb mode as an option (Nov 2025). — [TechCrunch, Sep 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/24/openai-rolls-out-advanced-voice-mode-with-more-voices-and-a-new-look); [TechCrunch, Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/25/chatgpts-voice-mode-is-no-longer-a-separate-interface/). Its speed comes from a single end-to-end model (232 ms minimum, 320 ms average). — [OpenAI: Hello GPT-4o](https://openai.com/index/hello-gpt-4o/). Community posts in 2026 ask for orb color customization and an animated avatar (feature requests, not shipped). — [OpenAI Community: Customize Voice Mode Orb Color](https://community.openai.com/t/feature-request-customize-voice-mode-orb-color/1396625.md) *(snippet-level)*

- **Gemini Live:** a circle/gradient identity with motion as a guiding element. — [Google Design](https://design.google/library/gemini-ai-visual-design). - Apple HIG: "the best haptic experience is one that people may not be conscious of, but miss when it's turned off." — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)

### Inferences
- **Premium markers for this app:**
  1. Instant, physical press response.
  2. A listening visual that visibly *proves* it hears the user (amplitude-reactive, smoothed).
  3. A crisp "stopped listening" moment.
  4. A processing state that feels short and purposeful, not a generic spinner.
  5. A success state that shows the *parsed result* (amount and category), so the user trusts what was saved.
  6. Haptics that match the visuals.
  7. Silence everywhere else.
- **Generic markers to avoid:** perpetual glow or orb animation at idle; system spinners; states that differ only by tint; showing "listening" before the mic is live; long celebratory success animations on a task repeated many times a day.
- The single-element monochrome concept is close to Wispr's "invisible but present" stance and Siri's "whole-surface as indicator" stance. Its premium feel will come mostly from motion quality (springs, smoothing, timing) and latency, not visual richness.
- The move by ChatGPT and Gemini from a separate orb screen toward inline voice with text suggests that, for task completion, *showing the content* (transcript or result) beats spectacle. For an expense logger, the result card is the payoff.

### Gaps
- I found no published interviews with the designers of ChatGPT voice, Gemini Live or Apple's Siri glow explaining their motion or state rationale (searches returned only press coverage).
- I found no expert critique (Substack, Figma blog, Medium) of these UIs that met the quality bar in the retrieved results; the comparison here is built from product docs, press and teardowns.
- I found no reliable sources on Humane's interface specifics (laser projector interaction, Trust Light) beyond general review summaries, and nothing on Siri visual changes in iOS 26.
